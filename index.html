What it is thinking

32 inputs (what it sees), two hidden layers, and one output per direction. The brightest output is the move it makes.

What it sees

From its head the snake looks in eight directions. For each one it knows how close the wall is, how close its own body is, and whether food lies that way.

How it decides

Those numbers go into a small neural network with two hidden layers. Its four outputs are up, right, down and left, and the snake follows the strongest.

How it learns

400 snakes with random brains play a game each. The best are more likely to become parents; each child mixes its parents' weights and some change at random. Repeat.
